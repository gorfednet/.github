What the numbers say:
- The pool was slot-bound before the remotes. Whether it still is with 8 slots isn't measured yet.
- BinderCurve is two thirds of the demand, and half its runs are a monitor cron.
- The Mac's gate time is small. The long part of every release test pass already runs on the pool, where it waits behind ordinary PR work.

## Options, ranked

ETAs are ET.

### 1. Re-measure with the remotes live

- **What.** Rerun `pool-wait-report.mjs --hours 48` once 48 h of remote placement have passed. Teach it the remote slots first (PR 670 adds `remote` to its reader), or it reads busy against 4 slots.
- **Value.** Every later item is sized on pre-remote numbers. This says whether 8 slots already meet row 3.1's done test (median under 10 min, p90 under 30).
- **Cost.** About 450 API calls (check `rate_limit` first). `runner` Haiku.
- **Risk.** None.
- **ETA.** 17 Nov, 18:00 ET.

### 2. Shrink BinderCurve's demand (plan row 3.10, plus `production-monitor`)

- **What.** Row 3.10's fail-closed "no app code" class. Move `production-monitor` off the heavy pool, or cut its cadence: it's half of BinderCurve's runs.
- **Value.** Frees slots for priorities 1 to 5 at once. Not sized: a 7-day read by path class comes first.
- **Cost.** Fable decides the class, a `builder` Sonnet builds it, Fable reviews the diff.
- **Risk.** A wrong class skips the browser gate on app code. Fail closed on any unmatched path.
- **ETA.** row 3.10 says after the pace hold lifts. Target 18 Nov.

### 3. A release lane in the pool

- **What.** `plan_starts` today fills fewest-held repository first, with no priority order. Give runs from a `release` pull request, then priorities 1 to 5, first claim on free slots across all 3 hosts. This is how the release test pass gets distributed: it runs on every machine before plain PR work, under the owner's placement order.
- **Value.** A release's browser pass stops waiting behind BinderCurve PRs. The passing BinderCurve attempt spent about 130 of its 230 min queued. Releases are at most 1 a week per repository, so the lane takes little from everyone else.
- **Cost.** A supervisor change: `builder` Sonnet with Fable on the design and the diff (it decides what runs unattended). A bindercurve.com PR through the full gate until row 3.11 moves the pool to ops.
- **Risk.** Starvation of BinderCurve PRs while a release runs. Cap the lane at the release's own shard count.
- **ETA.** 19 Nov.

### 4. Hosted overflow as the last resort

- **What.** Step 3 of the owner's placement order, already built and off on the placement branch above. `shipping.md` makes overflow automatic past a 15-min wait for priorities 1 to 5.
- **Value.** 4thcltr p90 87 min and MoonMan p90 105 sit well past 15 min.
- **Cost.** Hosted minutes, under the US$100 monthly cap. Owned by the session on that branch.
- **Risk.** Spend. The cap stops it.
- **ETA.** that session's PR. Not this plan's work.

### 5. Finish step 6 properly and size gowyllop's row

- **What.** Resume ops PR 99's harness with gowyllop drained from the pool for each run. Time BinderCurve `check` (tightest: 29 of 35 min), WebKit shard 5 and 4thcltr chromium shard 8, at N = 2, 3 and 4, 2 repeats.
- **Value.** Sets slots and cpus so no job nears its timeout on the slower cores. Items 7 and 8 depend on it.
- **Cost.** At least 7 h of gowyllop per pass (12 runs of 35 min or more), with its slots out of the pool meanwhile. `builder` Sonnet, since it judges a contaminated run. dapyllil is never quiet, so its timings come from pool job history instead.
- **Risk.** Lost CI capacity while drained. Run it overnight.
- **ETA.** 17 Nov.

### 6. Balance the shards

- **What.** Split by measured duration, not file count. 4thcltr's shards run 13 to 34 min, BinderCurve WebKit 13 to 37.
- **Value.** Once slots are free the longest shard is the floor. Balanced, 4thcltr's floor drops from 34 to about 20 min, BinderCurve WebKit from 37 to about 25.
- **Cost.** A `builder` Sonnet per repo. Playwright's `--shard` splits by file, so slow spec files get moved or split.
- **Risk.** Low. The executed-test floor must still hold.
- **ETA.** 18 Nov.

### 7. Repo caps, after 1 and 5

- **What.** Hold BinderCurve at 4. Raise 4thcltr 3 to 4 (it's the only repo with wait behind its own cap). Gate on gowyllop's measured row alone: gamingpc-ci has never answered. Batch with any other `pools.conf` change into one reinstall.
- **Value.** Raising BinderCurve buys little. At cap 4 its passing attempt models at about 100 min (29 + 283 / 4), at 6 about 85. Most of its 230 min was queue, so items 2 and 3 are worth more. 4thcltr at 4 wide needs at least 1 shard off dapyllil at matching speed.
- **Cost.** One line, a contract-test update, a reinstall.
- **Risk.** Higher caps on priority 6 starve priorities 1 to 5.
- **ETA.** 18 Nov.

### 8. The gaming PC

- **What.** It has taken 0 jobs. Before any boot or wake change, a reboot must drain first: a lost remote runner fails its job and nothing reruns it. That's a red PR check for a reason the PR can't change (verification floor, rule 5).
- **Value.** Up to 3 slots.
- **Cost.** A drain-before-reboot step or a rerun-on-runner-lost in the supervisor (`builder` Sonnet, Fable read). Then the owner's own setup on his PC, written in ops.
- **Risk.** It's the owner's machine first.
- **ETA.** 19 Nov for the drain. His setup after that.

### 9. Warm caches per repo

- **What.** Measure step times (checkout, `npm ci`, Playwright install, build) over 7 days of pool jobs, then bake in what dominates.
- **Value.** Unknown. The worst start-time cost, checkout stalls, has a fix in PR 655, not yet measured after install.
- **Cost.** About 300 API calls.
- **Risk.** A cache written by PR code can poison main and release runs. Mount it read-only, filled from main only.
- **ETA.** measurement 18 Nov.

### 10. Headless builders off the Mac (plan row 1.15)

- **What.** Row 1.15 as written: dapyllil, after rows 1.11 to 1.13.
- **Value.** The Mac ran at load 10 to 80. `builder-ok` holds aren't logged, so measure them first.
- **Cost.** Row 1.15's full setup and an owner sudo step.
- **Risk.** A builder's token on a second host widens what can push. gowyllop isn't a fit yet: its CI row alone reached load 30, and only browsers are proven on its CPU.
- **ETA.** after the 20 Nov reset.

### 11. Release gates off the Mac: not recommended

- **Value.** Small. All Mac gates took about 4 h in 8 days, the longest about 16 min, and releases are at most 1 a week per repository.
- **What the Mac gives.** A second environment that can't share a fault with the pool image, and the owner's 14 November ask.
- **What only macOS can run.** MoonMan's Xcode and device builds. Real Safari: nothing runs it today. The pool's WebKit is Playwright's Linux build.
- **Instead.** Add a load guard to release gates in `gate-run`: wait while the 1-minute load is above the core count. The heuristics release gate flaked once at 17:44 ET with the Mac heavily loaded, cause not established.

## The owner's decision

Keep release gates on this Mac, as `shipping.md` already says? Recommendation: yes. The Mac part is short. The time goes on the release's browser pass waiting in the pool queue, which item 3 fixes on our own machines.
